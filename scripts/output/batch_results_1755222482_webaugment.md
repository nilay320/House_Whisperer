## 1. Question

What does the InterNACHI SOP require for inspecting electrical panels?

### Answer

The InterNACHI Standards of Practice (SOP) outline specific requirements for inspecting electrical panels, which are supplemented by the regulations from the NC Home Inspector Licensure Board. Here are the key points regarding the inspection of electrical panels:

### Mandatory Requirements (from Regulatory Sources)

1. **Description of Electrical Service**: The home inspector must describe:
   - The electrical service amperage and voltage.
   - The electrical service entry conductor materials at the readily openable access panels at the main.
   - The electrical service type (overhead or underground).
   - The location of main and distribution panels (NC Home Inspector Licensure Board).

2. **Operation of Electrical Components**: The inspector is required to operate a representative number of installed ceiling fans, lighting fixtures, switches, and receptacles located inside the house, garage, and on the dwelling’s exterior walls (NC Home Inspector Licensure Board).

3. **Ground Fault Circuit Interrupters (GFCIs)**: The inspector must operate GFCIs located within six feet of the floor/ground (NC Home Inspector Licensure Board).

4. **Reporting Deficiencies**: The inspector must report deficiencies such as:
   - Integrity issues with service-entrance conductors’ insulation.
   - Unused circuit-breaker panel openings that are not filled.
   - Presence of solid conductor aluminum branch-circuit wiring, if visible.
   - Any tested receptacle with power issues, incorrect polarity, or grounding issues.
   - Absence of smoke and/or carbon monoxide detectors (InterNACHI SOP).

### Limitations on Inspection (from Regulatory Sources)

1. **No Insertion of Tools**: Inspectors are not required to insert any tool, probe, or device into the main panelboard or sub-panels (InterNACHI SOP; NC Home Inspector Licensure Board).

2. **No Dismantling**: Inspectors are not required to dismantle any electrical device or control, except to remove the covers of panelboard enclosures (NC Home Inspector Licensure Board).

3. **Testing Limitations**: Inspectors are not required to test or operate arc-fault circuit interrupters or other overcurrent devices, except for GFCIs (NC Home Inspector Licensure Board).

4. **Non-Energized Systems**: Inspectors are not required to activate any electrical systems or branch circuits that are not energized (InterNACHI SOP).

### Summary

In summary, the InterNACHI SOP and NC Home Inspector Licensure Board regulations require home inspectors to describe and operate certain electrical components while also reporting deficiencies. However, they are limited in their actions regarding the inspection of electrical panels, particularly in terms of inserting tools or dismantling components. 

For further details, you may refer to the InterNACHI Standards of Practice and the NC Home Inspector Licensure Board regulations.

## 2. Question

What are the continuing education requirements for NC home inspectors?

### Answer

In North Carolina, the continuing education requirements for home inspectors are outlined by the NC Home Inspector Licensure Board. Here are the key details:

1. **Credit Hours Required**: To renew an active home inspector license, a licensee must complete **12 credit hours** of continuing education during the license renewal period, which runs from **October 1 through the following September 30** (Source: NC Home Inspector Licensure Board, Section .1302).

2. **Additional Requirements for New Licensees**: Home inspectors who have not completed the pre-licensing education program or its equivalent must complete **16 credit hours** of continuing education per year for the first three years of licensure. This includes:
   - **Four hours** of a Board-approved course on the **Standards of Practice and Report Writing** in the first year.
   - **Four hours** of a Board-approved course of the home inspector's choosing in the second and third years.
   - **Four hours** of the update course component as described in Rule .1309 (Source: NC Home Inspector Licensure Board, Section .1302).

3. **Update Course Requirement**: To maintain an active license, a licensee must complete a Board-developed update course within one year (Source: NC Home Inspector Licensure Board, Section .1309).

These requirements ensure that home inspectors remain knowledgeable and up-to-date with industry standards and practices. For further details, you may refer to the NC Home Inspector Licensure Board's regulations directly.

## 5. Question

What are the best practices for roof inspections according to InterNACHI and current industry standards?

### Answer

### Best Practices for Roof Inspections According to InterNACHI and Current Industry Standards

#### Mandatory Requirements (Regulatory Sources)

According to the **InterNACHI Standards of Practice**, the following are mandatory requirements for roof inspections:

1. **Inspection Scope**:
   - Inspect from ground level or the eaves:
     - Roof-covering materials
     - Gutters
     - Downspouts
     - Vents, flashing, skylights, chimney, and other roof penetrations
     - General structure of the roof from readily accessible panels, doors, or stairs (Source: InterNACHI Standards of Practice).

2. **Reporting**:
   - Describe the type of roof-covering materials.
   - Report any observed indications of active roof leaks as needing correction (Source: InterNACHI Standards of Practice).

3. **Limitations**:
   - Inspectors are **not required** to:
     - Walk on any roof surface.
     - Predict the service life expectancy of the roof.
     - Inspect underground downspout diverter drainage pipes.
     - Remove snow, ice, debris, or other conditions that prohibit observation.
     - Move insulation or inspect certain attachments (e.g., antennae, satellite dishes) (Source: InterNACHI Standards of Practice).

#### Recommended Practices (Web Resources)

From the **InterNACHI guide on how to perform roof inspections**, the following best practices are recommended:

1. **Identification**:
   - Identify the roof covering material and report any visible deficiencies.
   - Recommend repairs and/or maintenance as needed.

2. **Gutter Inspection**:
   - Ensure that all gutters slope toward the downspout. Look for signs of standing water in portions of the gutter away from the downspout (Source: InterNACHI: [How to Perform Roof Inspections](https://www.nachi.org/documents2012/How_to_Perform_Roof_Inspections_THIRD_EDITION-revd-Dec-2013.pdf)).

3. **Safety Considerations**:
   - Avoid actions that may be unsafe, such as walking on roof surfaces or climbing ladders if conditions appear hazardous (Source: InterNACHI Standards of Practice).

4. **Documentation**:
   - Document findings clearly, focusing on material defects observed on the date of the inspection, without predicting future conditions (Source: InterNACHI Standards of Practice).

### Conclusion

In summary, roof inspections should adhere to the mandatory requirements set forth by InterNACHI, focusing on thorough visual inspections from safe vantage points and accurate reporting of findings. Recommended practices emphasize the importance of identifying materials, ensuring proper drainage, and maintaining safety throughout the inspection process. For further details, refer to the InterNACHI Standards of Practice and their guide on performing roof inspections.

## 7. Question

What are the inspection requirements for electrical, plumbing, and structural components in North Carolina?

### Answer

In North Carolina, the inspection requirements for electrical, plumbing, and structural components are outlined in the NC Home Inspector Licensure Board regulations and the NC Building Codes. Here’s a summary of the mandatory requirements for each category:

### Electrical Inspection Requirements
According to the NC Home Inspector Licensure Board, home inspectors are required to inspect the following electrical components:
1. **Electrical Service Entrance Conductors**: Inspect for proper installation and condition.
2. **Electrical Service Equipment**: This includes grounding equipment, the main overcurrent device, and the interiors of panelboard enclosures, unless unsafe conditions are reported.
3. **Amperage and Voltage Ratings**: Inspect the ratings of the electrical service.
4. **Branch Circuit Conductors**: Inspect their overcurrent devices and ensure compatibility of their ampacities at the interiors of panelboard enclosures, unless unsafe conditions are reported.
5. **Operation of Installed Fixtures**: Inspect a representative number of installed ceiling fans, lighting fixtures, and switches (NC Home Inspector Licensure Board, .1110 ELECTRICAL).

### Plumbing Inspection Requirements
The NC Home Inspector Licensure Board mandates that home inspectors inspect:
1. **Pipes**: Ensure they are visible and in good condition.
2. **Water Heater Capacity**: Report on the adequacy of the reported water heater capacity.
3. **Thermal Expansion Tanks**: Report on their presence or absence.
4. **Sewage Pumps**: Inspect the operation of sewage pumps (NC Home Inspector Licensure Board, .1109 PLUMBING).

### Structural Inspection Requirements
The NC Building Codes specify that certain structural components must be visible for inspection, including:
1. **Pipes, Chimneys, and Vents**: These must be accessible for inspection.
2. **Flashing for Roofs and Chimneys**: Must be in place and visible.
3. **Insulation Baffles**: Should be installed correctly.
4. **Lintels**: Required to be bolted to framing for support and must not be covered by any wall or ceiling finish material before approval (NC Building Codes 2024).

Additionally, special inspections may be required for certain types of work, which must be performed by a North Carolina registered design professional or an inspector under their responsible charge (NC Building Codes 2024, Section 1704).

### Conclusion
Home inspectors in North Carolina must adhere to the specific requirements set forth by the NC Home Inspector Licensure Board and the NC Building Codes for electrical, plumbing, and structural components. These inspections are crucial for ensuring the safety and compliance of residential properties. For further details, refer to the NC Home Inspector Licensure Board and NC Building Codes documentation.

## 8. Question

What are the latest best practices for inspecting heat pumps, including InterNACHI standards, NC requirements, and manufacturer recommendations?

### Answer

### Best Practices for Inspecting Heat Pumps in North Carolina

#### Mandatory Requirements

1. **Inspection of Heating Systems**: According to the NC Home Inspector Licensure Board, home inspectors must inspect permanently installed heating systems, which include:
   - Heating equipment
   - Normal operating controls
   - Automatic safety controls
   - Heat distribution systems (fans, pumps, ducts, piping, etc.)
   - The presence of an installed heat source for each habitable space (NC General Statutes and NC Administrative Code, .1111 HEATING).

2. **Operational Testing**: Inspectors are required to operate the heating systems using normal operating controls appropriate to the weather conditions at the time of the inspection (NC General Statutes and NC Administrative Code, .1111 HEATING).

3. **Description Requirements**: Inspectors must describe the energy source and the type of heating equipment and distribution system present (NC General Statutes and NC Administrative Code, .1111 HEATING).

4. **Compliance with Building Codes**: The NC Building Codes outline specific requirements for heat pump equipment, including installation and performance standards (NC Building Codes 2024).

#### Recommended Best Practices

1. **General Inspection Practices**: According to InterNACHI standards, inspectors should:
   - Not move personal items or obstructions that may restrict the visual inspection.
   - Avoid dismantling or uncovering any system or component during the inspection (InterNACHI: Home Inspection Standards of Practice - [InterNACHI SOP](http://www.internachi.org/sop)).

2. **Operational Checks**: Ensure that the heating and cooling systems are turned off during testing, and that supply and return registers are not sealed (NC Building Codes 2024).

3. **Documentation**: Maintain thorough documentation of the inspection, including any air leakage information and the conditions observed during the inspection (NC Building Codes 2024).

4. **Manufacturer Recommendations**: While specific manufacturer recommendations are not detailed in the provided sources, it is advisable to consult the manufacturer's guidelines for specific operational checks and maintenance practices relevant to the heat pump model being inspected.

### Conclusion

In summary, home inspectors in North Carolina must adhere to specific regulatory requirements regarding the inspection of heat pumps, including operational testing and documentation. Additionally, following best practices from InterNACHI can enhance the thoroughness of the inspection process. Always refer to manufacturer guidelines for model-specific recommendations.
