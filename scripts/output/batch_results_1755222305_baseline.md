## 1. Question

What does the InterNACHI SOP require for inspecting electrical panels?

### Answer

The InterNACHI Standards of Practice (SOP) outline specific requirements for inspecting electrical panels, which include both mandatory requirements and limitations on what inspectors are required to do.

### Mandatory Requirements:
1. **Description of Electrical Service**: The inspector must describe:
   - The main service disconnect's amperage rating, if labeled.
   - The type of wiring observed (InterNACHI SOP II.A).
   
2. **Reporting Deficiencies**: The inspector is required to report as needing correction:
   - Deficiencies in the integrity of service-entrance conductors’ insulation, drip loop, and vertical clearances from grade and roofs.
   - Any unused circuit-breaker panel openings that were not filled.
   - The presence of solid conductor aluminum branch-circuit wiring, if readily visible.
   - Any tested receptacle where power was not present, polarity was incorrect, the cover was not in place, GFCI devices were improperly installed or did not operate, evidence of arcing or excessive heat, and where the receptacle was not grounded or secured to the wall.
   - The absence of smoke and/or carbon monoxide detectors (InterNACHI SOP III).

### Limitations:
The inspector is **not required** to:
- Insert any tool, probe, or device into the main panelboard, sub-panels, or distribution panelboards (InterNACHI SOP IV.A).
- Test or operate any arc-fault circuit interrupters or other overcurrent devices, except for ground fault circuit interrupters (NC Home Inspector Licensure Board).
- Dismantle any electrical device or control other than to remove the covers of panelboard enclosures (NC Home Inspector Licensure Board).
- Inspect low voltage systems, security systems, or other ancillary wiring that is not part of the primary electrical distribution system (NC Home Inspector Licensure Board).

### Summary:
In summary, while the InterNACHI SOP requires inspectors to describe certain aspects of electrical panels and report deficiencies, it also sets clear boundaries on what inspectors are not obligated to do, such as inserting tools into panels or testing non-GFCI devices. These guidelines ensure that inspections are thorough yet safe and within the scope of the inspector's responsibilities. 

For further details, you may refer to the InterNACHI Standards of Practice and the NC Home Inspector Licensure Board regulations.

What are the continuing education requirements for NC home inspectors?

### Answer

In North Carolina, the continuing education requirements for home inspectors are outlined by the NC Home Inspector Licensure Board. To renew an active home inspector license, the following requirements must be met:

1. **General Requirement**: Licensees must complete **12 credit hours** of continuing education during the license renewal period, which runs from **October 1 through the following September 30** (Source: NC Home Inspector Licensure Board, Section .1302).

2. **Additional Requirement for New Licensees**: Home inspectors who have not completed the pre-licensing education program or its equivalent must complete **16 credit hours** of continuing education per year for the first three years of licensure. This includes:
   - **Four hours** of a Board-approved course:
     - For the first year: A course on the **Standards of Practice and Report Writing**.
     - For the second and third years: A Board-approved course of the inspector's choosing.
   - **Four hours** of the update course component as described in Rule .1309 (Source: NC Home Inspector Licensure Board, Section .1302).

3. **Update Course Requirement**: To maintain an active license status, licensees are also required to complete a Board-developed update course within one year (Source: NC Home Inspector Licensure Board, Section .1309).

### Answer

In North Carolina, the inspection requirements for electrical, plumbing, and structural components are governed by the NC Home Inspector Licensure Board and the NC Building Codes. Below is a summary of the mandatory inspection requirements for each category:

### Electrical Inspection Requirements
According to the NC Home Inspector Licensure Board, home inspectors are required to inspect the following electrical components:
1. **Electrical service entrance conductors**
2. **Electrical service equipment**, including grounding equipment, main overcurrent devices, and interiors of panelboard enclosures (unless unsafe conditions are reported)
3. **Amperage and voltage ratings** of the electrical service
4. **Branch circuit conductors**, their overcurrent devices, and the compatibility of their ampacities at the interiors of panelboard enclosures (unless unsafe conditions are reported)
5. **Operation of a representative number** of installed ceiling fans, lighting fixtures, switches, and other electrical equipment (NC Home Inspector Licensure Board, October 1, 2024).

### Plumbing Inspection Requirements
The NC Home Inspector Licensure Board mandates that home inspectors inspect:
1. **Pipes**
2. **Chimneys and vents**
3. **Flashing for roofs and chimneys**
4. **Insulation baffles**
5. **Lintels** that are required to be bolted to the framing for support, which must not be covered by any exterior or interior wall or ceiling finish material before approval (NC Building Codes 2024).

### Structural Inspection Requirements
For structural components, the NC Building Codes specify that:
1. **Sheathing inspections** are required for structures in wind zones of 140 mph or higher where the exterior sheathing is part of the lateral force resistance system. These inspections must occur during or after the framing inspection but before the water-resistant barrier is applied (NC Building Codes 2024).
2. **Special inspections** may be required for certain types of work, which must be performed by a North Carolina registered design professional or an inspector under their responsible charge (NC Building Codes 2024).

In summary, home inspectors in North Carolina must adhere to specific inspection requirements for electrical, plumbing, and structural components as outlined by the NC Home Inspector Licensure Board and the NC Building Codes.
