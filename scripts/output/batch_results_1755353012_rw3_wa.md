## 1. Question

What does the InterNACHI SOP require for inspecting electrical panels?

### Answer

The InterNACHI Standards of Practice (SOP) outline specific requirements for inspecting electrical panels, which include both mandatory requirements and limitations on what inspectors are expected to do.

### Mandatory Requirements:
1. **Description of Electrical Components**: Inspectors are required to describe:
   - The main service disconnect's amperage rating, if labeled.
   - The type of wiring observed (InterNACHI SOP II.A).

2. **Reporting Deficiencies**: Inspectors must report as needing correction:
   - Deficiencies in the integrity of service-entrance conductors’ insulation, drip loop, and vertical clearances.
   - Any unused circuit-breaker panel openings that are not filled.
   - The presence of solid conductor aluminum branch-circuit wiring, if readily visible.
   - Issues with tested receptacles, such as lack of power, incorrect polarity, or improper installation of GFCI devices.
   - The absence of smoke and/or carbon monoxide detectors (InterNACHI SOP III).

### Limitations:
Inspectors are **not required** to:
- Insert any tool, probe, or device into the main panelboard or sub-panels.
- Test or operate arc-fault circuit interrupters or other overcurrent devices, except for ground fault circuit interrupters.
- Dismantle any electrical device or control, other than removing the covers of panelboard enclosures.
- Inspect low voltage systems, security systems, or other ancillary wiring that is not part of the primary electrical distribution system (InterNACHI SOP IV).

### Summary:
In summary, while home inspectors must describe certain aspects of electrical panels and report deficiencies, they are limited in their actions regarding the inspection of these panels. They cannot perform invasive inspections or operate certain electrical devices. This aligns with the regulatory requirements set forth by the NC Home Inspector Licensure Board, which also emphasizes the importance of safety and accessibility in inspections.

For further details, you can refer to the InterNACHI Standards of Practice and the NC Home Inspector Licensure Board guidelines.

## 2. Question

What are the continuing education requirements for NC home inspectors?

### Answer

In North Carolina, the continuing education requirements for home inspectors are outlined by the NC Home Inspector Licensure Board. Here are the key details:

1. **Credit Hours Required**: To renew an active home inspector license, a licensee must complete **12 credit hours** of continuing education during the license renewal period, which runs from **October 1 through the following September 30** (Source: NC Home Inspector Licensure Board, Section .1302).

2. **Additional Requirements for New Licensees**: Home inspectors who are licensed under G.S. 143-151.51(a)(5) and have not completed the pre-licensing education program must complete **16 credit hours** of continuing education per year for the first three years of licensure. This includes:
   - **Four hours** of a Board-approved course on the Standards of Practice and Report Writing in the first year.
   - **Four hours** of a Board-approved course of the home inspector's choosing in the second and third years.
   - **Four hours** of the update course component as described in Rule .1309 (Source: NC Home Inspector Licensure Board, Section .1302).

3. **Update Course Requirement**: To maintain an active license, a licensee must complete a Board-developed update course within one year (Source: NC Home Inspector Licensure Board, Section .1309).

These requirements ensure that home inspectors remain knowledgeable about current practices and regulations in the field. For further details, you may refer to the NC Home Inspector Licensure Board's regulations.

What are the NC building code requirements for deck railing height?

### Answer

In North Carolina, the building code requirements for deck railing height specify that the railing must be a minimum of 36 inches (914 mm) high. Additionally, it must provide a minimum diagonal measurement of 42 inches (1067 mm) from the top of the rail to the nosing of the nearest tread (NC Building Codes 2024).

## 5. Question

What are the best practices for roof inspections according to InterNACHI and current industry standards?

### Answer

### Best Practices for Roof Inspections According to InterNACHI and Current Industry Standards

#### Mandatory Requirements (Regulatory Sources)

According to the **InterNACHI Standards of Practice**, the following requirements must be adhered to during roof inspections:

1. **Inspection Scope**:
   - Inspect from ground level or the eaves:
     - Roof-covering materials
     - Gutters
     - Downspouts
     - Vents, flashing, skylights, chimney, and other roof penetrations
     - General structure of the roof from readily accessible panels, doors, or stairs (Source: InterNACHI Standards of Practice).

2. **Reporting**:
   - Describe the type of roof-covering materials.
   - Report any observed indications of active roof leaks as needing correction.

3. **Limitations**:
   - Inspectors are **not required** to:
     - Walk on any roof surface.
     - Predict the service life expectancy of the roof.
     - Inspect underground downspout diverter drainage pipes.
     - Remove snow, ice, debris, or other conditions that prohibit observation of roof surfaces.
     - Move insulation or inspect certain attachments (e.g., antennae, satellite dishes) (Source: InterNACHI Standards of Practice).

#### Recommended Practices (Web Resources)

From the **InterNACHI guide on performing roof inspections**, the following best practices are recommended:

1. **Identify Roof Covering Material**:
   - Always identify the type of roof covering material during the inspection.

2. **Visible Deficiencies**:
   - Report any visible deficiencies observed during the inspection.

3. **Recommendations**:
   - Recommend necessary repairs and/or maintenance based on the findings.

4. **Avoid Predictions**:
   - Do not report on the future life expectancy of the roof covering.

5. **Gutter Inspection**:
   - Ensure that all gutters slope toward the downspout and check for signs of standing water, which may indicate improper installation (Source: InterNACHI How to Perform Roof Inspections).

6. **Safety Considerations**:
   - Avoid any actions that may be unsafe, such as walking on roof surfaces that appear unstable or could cause damage (Source: InterNACHI Standards of Practice).

### Conclusion

In summary, roof inspections should be conducted following the mandatory requirements set forth by InterNACHI, focusing on visible conditions and necessary repairs while adhering to safety protocols. Recommended practices emphasize thorough identification and reporting of roof conditions without making predictions about future performance. For further details, refer to the InterNACHI Standards of Practice [here](http://www.internachi.org/sop) and the guide on performing roof inspections [here](https://www.nachi.org/documents2012/How_to_Perform_Roof_Inspections_THIRD_EDITION-revd-Dec-2013.pdf).

What are recent updates to NC home inspector licensing requirements?

### Answer

Recent updates to North Carolina home inspector licensing requirements include several key components as outlined by the NC Home Inspector Licensure Board:

1. **Continuing Education Requirements**: Home inspectors must complete 12 hours of continuing education every 12 months. This includes a mandatory 4-hour Board-developed update course that must be taken live from an approved sponsor and instructor. The remaining 8 hours can be elective courses, and all 12 hours can be completed online (Source: McKissock, [North Carolina Home Inspection License Requirements](https://www.mckissock.com/home-inspection/continuing-education/north-carolina/requirements/)).

2. **Update Course Component**: To maintain an active license, licensees are required to complete a Board-developed update course within one year. This course is essential for license renewal and is designed to ensure that inspectors are up-to-date with current practices and regulations (Source: NC Home Inspector Licensure Board, October 1, 2024).

3. **Licensure Eligibility**: To be eligible for licensure, applicants must submit a completed application, pass a licensing examination, pay applicable fees, and meet educational requirements. Specifically, they must have a high school diploma or equivalent and complete an education program approved by the Board within three years of applying (Source: NC Home Inspector Licensure Board, October 1, 2024).

4. **Written Contract Requirement**: Licensed home inspectors must provide a written contract to clients, stating that the inspection will be performed in accordance with the North Carolina Home Inspector Licensure Board Standard of Practice. This contract must include the inspector’s name and license number (Source: NCREC Bulletins – 2025, [NCREC Bulletins](https://bulletins.ncrec.gov/2025/page/3/)).

These updates reflect the ongoing efforts to enhance the professionalism and accountability of home inspectors in North Carolina. For further details, home inspectors should refer to the NC Home Inspector Licensure Board's official publications and resources.

## 7. Question

What are the inspection requirements for electrical, plumbing, and structural components in North Carolina?

### Answer

In North Carolina, the inspection requirements for electrical, plumbing, and structural components are outlined in the NC Home Inspector Licensure Board regulations and the NC Building Codes. Here’s a summary of the key requirements:

### Electrical Inspection Requirements
According to the NC Home Inspector Licensure Board, home inspectors must inspect the following electrical components:
1. **Electrical Service Entrance Conductors** - Inspect for proper installation and condition.
2. **Electrical Service Equipment** - This includes grounding equipment, the main overcurrent device, and the interiors of panelboard enclosures, unless unsafe conditions are present.
3. **Amperage and Voltage Ratings** - Inspect the ratings of the electrical service.
4. **Branch Circuit Conductors** - Inspect their overcurrent devices and ensure compatibility of their ampacities within panelboard enclosures, unless unsafe conditions are reported.
5. **Operation of Installed Fixtures** - Inspect a representative number of installed ceiling fans, lighting fixtures, and switches. 

*(Source: NC Home Inspector Licensure Board, October 1, 2024)*

### Plumbing Inspection Requirements
The plumbing inspection requirements as per the NC Home Inspector Licensure Board include:
1. **Water Supply and Distribution Systems** - Inspect for proper installation and condition.
2. **Drainage, Waste, and Vent Systems** - Inspect for proper installation and condition.
3. **Fixtures and Faucets** - Inspect for proper operation and condition.
4. **Water Heaters** - Inspect for proper installation, including the adequacy of reported water heater capacity and the presence of thermal expansion tanks.

*(Source: NC Home Inspector Licensure Board, October 1, 2024)*

### Structural Inspection Requirements
For structural components, the NC Building Codes specify that the following items must be visible for inspection:
1. **Pipes** - Must be accessible for inspection.
2. **Chimneys and Vents** - Should be inspected for proper installation.
3. **Flashing for Roofs and Chimneys** - Must be visible and properly installed.
4. **Insulation Baffles** - Should be in place and visible.
5. **Lintels** - Required to be bolted to framing for support and must not be covered by any wall or ceiling finish material before approval.

Additionally, special inspections may be required for certain types of work, and these must be performed by a North Carolina registered design professional or an inspector under their responsible charge.

*(Source: NC Building Codes 2024)*

### Summary
In summary, home inspectors in North Carolina are required to conduct thorough inspections of electrical, plumbing, and structural components, adhering to the standards set forth by the NC Home Inspector Licensure Board and the NC Building Codes. These inspections ensure that systems are properly installed, functional, and compliant with safety regulations.

### Best Practices for Inspecting Heat Pumps in North Carolina

#### Mandatory Requirements

According to the **NC Home Inspector Licensure Board** regulations, home inspectors are required to perform the following when inspecting heating systems, including heat pumps:

1. **Inspect Permanently Installed Heating Systems**:
   - Heating equipment
   - Normal operating controls
   - Automatic safety controls
   - Chimneys, flues, and vents (where readily visible)
   - Heat distribution systems (including fans, pumps, ducts, piping, supports, insulation, air filters, registers, radiators, fan coil units, convectors)
   - Presence or absence of an installed heat source for each habitable space

2. **Describe Key Elements**:
   - Energy source
   - Heating equipment and distribution type

3. **Operate Systems**:
   - Inspectors must operate the systems using normal operating controls appropriate to the weather conditions at the time of the inspection (NC Home Inspector Licensure Board, October 1, 2024).

#### Recommended Practices

From **InterNACHI's Home Inspection Standards of Practice**, the following best practices are recommended for inspecting heat pumps:

1. **Accessibility**:
   - Inspectors should not move personal items or obstructions that may restrict the visual inspection.

2. **Limitations**:
   - Inspectors are not required to dismantle, open, or uncover any system or component, nor enter areas deemed unsafe.

3. **Documentation**:
   - Inspectors should document the condition of the heat pump and any ancillary systems, noting any visible issues or concerns.

4. **Testing**:
   - While specific testing protocols for heat pumps are not detailed, it is advisable to check for proper operation and any unusual noises or performance issues during the inspection.

5. **Compliance**:
   - Inspectors should be aware of compliance with local codes and regulations, although they are not responsible for determining compliance (InterNACHI, [Home Inspection Standards of Practice](http://www.internachi.org/sop)).

### Conclusion

In summary, when inspecting heat pumps in North Carolina, home inspectors must adhere to the mandatory requirements set forth by the NC Home Inspector Licensure Board, ensuring a thorough examination of all relevant components and systems. Additionally, following the recommended practices from InterNACHI can enhance the quality and thoroughness of the inspection. Inspectors should always stay updated on both regulatory changes and best practices to ensure compliance and provide the best service to clients.
