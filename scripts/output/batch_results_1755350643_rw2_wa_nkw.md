## 1. Question

What does the InterNACHI SOP require for inspecting electrical panels?

### Answer

The InterNACHI Standards of Practice (SOP) outline specific requirements for inspecting electrical panels, which include both mandatory requirements and limitations on what inspectors are expected to do.

### Mandatory Requirements:
1. **Description of Electrical Components**:
   - Inspectors must describe the main service disconnect's amperage rating, if labeled, and the type of wiring observed (InterNACHI SOP II.A).
   - Inspectors are required to report deficiencies such as:
     - Integrity issues with service-entrance conductors’ insulation, drip loop, and vertical clearances (InterNACHI SOP III.A).
     - Unused circuit-breaker panel openings that are not filled (InterNACHI SOP III.B).
     - Presence of solid conductor aluminum branch-circuit wiring, if readily visible (InterNACHI SOP III.C).
     - Issues with tested receptacles, including lack of power, incorrect polarity, improper installation of GFCI devices, evidence of arcing or excessive heat, and grounding issues (InterNACHI SOP III.D).
     - Absence of smoke and/or carbon monoxide detectors (InterNACHI SOP III.E).

### Limitations:
- Inspectors are **not required** to:
  - Insert any tool, probe, or device into the main panelboard or sub-panels (InterNACHI SOP IV.A).
  - Test or operate any arc-fault circuit interrupters or other overcurrent devices, except for ground fault circuit interrupters (InterNACHI SOP IV.B).
  - Dismantle any electrical device or control, other than removing the covers of panelboard enclosures (InterNACHI SOP IV.C).
  - Inspect low voltage systems, security systems, or other ancillary wiring not part of the primary electrical distribution system (InterNACHI SOP IV.D).

### Summary:
In summary, while home inspectors are required to describe certain aspects of electrical panels and report deficiencies, they are not obligated to perform invasive inspections or tests on the electrical systems. This aligns with the regulatory framework provided by the NC Home Inspector Licensure Board, which emphasizes safety and the limitations of inspection practices.

For further details, you can refer to the InterNACHI Standards of Practice and the NC Home Inspector Licensure Board guidelines.

## 2. Question

What are the continuing education requirements for NC home inspectors?

### Answer

To renew an active home inspector license in North Carolina, the following continuing education requirements must be met:

1. **General Requirement**: Licensees must complete **12 credit hours** of continuing education during the license renewal period, which runs from **October 1 through the following September 30** (NC Home Inspector Licensure Board, Section .1302(a)).

2. **Additional Requirement for New Licensees**: Home inspectors who are licensed under G.S. 143-151.51(a)(5) and have not completed the pre-licensing education program or its equivalent must complete **16 credit hours** of continuing education per year for the first three years of licensure. This includes:
   - **Four hours** of a Board-approved course:
     - For the first year: a course on the **Standards of Practice and Report Writing**.
     - For the second and third years: a Board-approved course of the inspector's choosing.
   - **Four hours** of the update course component as described in Rule .1309 (NC Home Inspector Licensure Board, Section .1302(b)).

3. **Update Course Requirement**: To maintain an active license, licensees must also complete a Board-developed update course within one year (NC Home Inspector Licensure Board, Section .1309(a)).

These requirements ensure that home inspectors stay informed about current practices and regulations in the field. For further details, you can refer to the NC Home Inspector Licensure Board's regulations.

## 3. Question

What are the NC building code requirements for deck railing height?

### Answer

In North Carolina, the building code requirements for deck railing height specify that the railing must be a minimum of 36 inches (914 mm) high. Additionally, it must provide a minimum diagonal measurement of 42 inches (1067 mm) from the top of the rail to the nosing of the nearest tread (NC Building Codes 2024).

For further details, refer to the following sections of the NC Building Codes 2024:
- Railing height: Minimum 36 inches (914 mm) above the floor or grade below.
- Diagonal measurement: Minimum 42 inches (1067 mm) from the top of the rail to the nearest tread nosing.

These requirements ensure safety and compliance for deck constructions in North Carolina.

## 5. Question

What are the best practices for roof inspections according to InterNACHI and current industry standards?

### Answer

### Best Practices for Roof Inspections According to InterNACHI and Current Industry Standards

#### Mandatory Requirements (Regulatory Sources)

According to the **InterNACHI Standards of Practice**, the following requirements must be adhered to during roof inspections:

1. **Inspection Scope**:
   - Inspect from ground level or the eaves:
     - Roof-covering materials
     - Gutters
     - Downspouts
     - Vents, flashing, skylights, chimney, and other roof penetrations
     - General structure of the roof from readily accessible panels, doors, or stairs (InterNACHI Standards of Practice, Section 3.1).

2. **Description and Reporting**:
   - Describe the type of roof-covering materials.
   - Report any observed indications of active roof leaks as needing correction (InterNACHI Standards of Practice, Section 3.1).

3. **Limitations**:
   - Inspectors are not required to:
     - Walk on any roof surface.
     - Predict the service life expectancy of the roof.
     - Inspect underground downspout diverter drainage pipes.
     - Remove snow, ice, debris, or other conditions that prohibit observation of roof surfaces.
     - Move insulation or inspect certain attachments (e.g., antennae, satellite dishes) (InterNACHI Standards of Practice, Section 3.1).

#### Recommended Practices (Web Resources)

From the **InterNACHI guide on performing roof inspections**, the following best practices are recommended:

1. **Identify Roof Covering Material**:
   - Always identify the type of roof covering material during the inspection.

2. **Visible Deficiencies**:
   - Report on any visible deficiencies found during the inspection.

3. **Recommendations**:
   - Recommend repairs and/or maintenance as needed based on the findings.

4. **Avoid Predictions**:
   - Do not report on the future life expectancy of the roof covering.

5. **Gutter Inspection**:
   - Ensure that gutters slope toward the downspout and check for signs of standing water, which may indicate improper installation (InterNACHI: How to Perform Roof Inspections).

6. **Safety Considerations**:
   - Avoid any actions that may be unsafe, such as walking on roof surfaces or climbing ladders if conditions appear hazardous (InterNACHI Standards of Practice).

For further details, you can refer to the full documents:
- [InterNACHI Standards of Practice](http://www.internachi.org/sop)
- [How to Perform Roof Inspections](https://www.nachi.org/documents2012/How_to_Perform_Roof_Inspections_THIRD_EDITION-revd-Dec-2013.pdf)

By following these mandatory requirements and recommended practices, home inspectors can ensure a thorough and safe roof inspection process.

What are recent updates to NC home inspector licensing requirements?

### Answer

Recent updates to North Carolina home inspector licensing requirements include several key components as outlined by the NC Home Inspector Licensure Board (NCHILB):

1. **Continuing Education Requirements**: 
   - Home inspectors must complete a Board-developed update course annually to maintain their license on active status. This update course provides four (4) hours of the total twelve (12) hours of continuing education (CE) required for license renewal by September 30th each year (Source: NCHILB, October 1, 2024).
   - If continuing education credit is denied or withdrawn, the licensee can request a 60-day extension to satisfy the requirement, but they remain responsible for meeting the CE requirements (Source: NCHILB, October 1, 2024).

2. **Licensure Eligibility**:
   - To be eligible for licensure, applicants must submit a completed application, pass a licensing examination, pay applicable fees, and meet one of the educational or experiential conditions set by the Board. This includes having a high school diploma or equivalent and completing an education program approved by the Board within three years of application submission (Source: NCHILB, October 1, 2024).

3. **Update Course Details**:
   - The NCHILB has developed an update course for the 2025-2026 period, which is mandatory for all licensed home inspectors. This course must be taken live from an approved sponsor and instructor (Source: Trainwithcasa, 2025 NC Update Training).

4. **Regulatory Framework**:
   - The NCHILB provides regulatory guidance through the NC General Statutes and Administrative Code, which includes changes noted in the latest publication effective October 1, 2024 (Source: NCHILB, October 1, 2024).

## 7. Question

What are the inspection requirements for electrical, plumbing, and structural components in North Carolina?

### Answer

In North Carolina, the inspection requirements for electrical, plumbing, and structural components are governed by the NC Home Inspector Licensure Board and the NC Building Codes. Here’s a summary of the mandatory requirements for each category:

### Electrical Inspection Requirements
According to the NC Home Inspector Licensure Board, home inspectors are required to inspect the following electrical components:
1. **Electrical service entrance conductors**
2. **Electrical service equipment**, including grounding equipment, main overcurrent devices, and interiors of panelboard enclosures (unless unsafe conditions are reported)
3. **Amperage and voltage ratings** of the electrical service
4. **Branch circuit conductors**, their overcurrent devices, and the compatibility of their ampacities at the interiors of panelboard enclosures (unless unsafe conditions are reported)
5. **Operation of a representative number** of installed ceiling fans, lighting fixtures, switches, and other electrical equipment (NC Home Inspector Licensure Board, .1110 ELECTRICAL).

### Plumbing Inspection Requirements
The plumbing inspection requirements as outlined by the NC Home Inspector Licensure Board include:
1. Inspecting the **water supply and distribution systems**
2. Inspecting the **drain, waste, and vent systems**
3. Reporting on the **absence or presence of thermal expansion tanks**
4. Reporting on the **adequacy of the reported water heater capacity**
5. Operating **sewage pumps** (NC Home Inspector Licensure Board, .1109 PLUMBING).

### Structural Inspection Requirements
For structural components, the NC Building Codes specify that the following items must be visible for inspection:
1. **Pipes**
2. **Chimneys and vents**
3. **Flashing for roofs and chimneys**
4. **Insulation baffles**
5. **Lintels** that are required to be bolted to the framing for support must not be covered by any wall or ceiling finish material before approval (NC Building Codes 2024).

Additionally, special inspections may be required for certain types of work, and these must be performed by a North Carolina registered design professional or an inspector under their responsible charge (NC Building Codes 2024, Section 1704).

### Summary
In summary, home inspectors in North Carolina must adhere to specific inspection requirements for electrical, plumbing, and structural components as mandated by the NC Home Inspector Licensure Board and the NC Building Codes. These inspections ensure that systems are properly sized, designed, and functioning, and that structural elements are adequately supported and visible for assessment. 

For further details, refer to the NC Home Inspector Licensure Board and NC Building Codes documentation.

## 8. Question

What are the latest best practices for inspecting heat pumps, including InterNACHI standards, NC requirements, and manufacturer recommendations?

### Answer

### Best Practices for Inspecting Heat Pumps

#### Mandatory Requirements

According to the **NC Home Inspector Licensure Board** regulations, home inspectors in North Carolina are required to inspect the following components of permanently installed heating systems, including heat pumps:

1. **Heating Equipment**: Inspect the physical condition and functionality of the heat pump.
2. **Normal Operating Controls**: Operate the system using normal controls appropriate to the weather conditions at the time of inspection.
3. **Automatic Safety Controls**: Check for the presence and functionality of safety controls.
4. **Chimneys, Flues, and Vents**: Inspect these components where readily visible.
5. **Heat Distribution Systems**: Evaluate fans, pumps, ducts, piping, and their supports, insulation, air filters, registers, radiators, fan coil units, and convectors.
6. **Installed Heat Source**: Confirm the presence or absence of an installed heat source for each habitable space.

Additionally, inspectors must describe:
- The energy source.
- The type of heating equipment and distribution system used (NC Home Inspector Licensure Board, October 1, 2024).

#### Recommended Practices

From **InterNACHI's Home Inspection Standards of Practice**, the following best practices are recommended for inspecting heat pumps:

1. **Accessibility**: Ensure that all components are readily accessible for inspection. Inspectors should not move personal items or obstructions that may restrict the visual inspection.
2. **Operational Testing**: Operate the heat pump under normal conditions to assess functionality. This includes checking the heating and cooling modes, if applicable.
3. **Documentation**: Document the condition of the heat pump, including any visible signs of wear or malfunction, and note any recommendations for further evaluation by a licensed HVAC professional.
4. **Safety Checks**: Inspect for gas or fuel leaks and indications thereof, ensuring that safety is prioritized during the inspection process.

#### Additional Considerations

- **Building Codes Compliance**: Inspectors should be aware of the NC Building Codes, which may require calculations for the performance of heating and cooling equipment based on climate and equipment sizing (NC Building Codes 2024).
- **Testing Conditions**: During testing, ensure that exterior windows and doors are closed but not sealed, and that dampers are closed but not sealed. Heating and cooling systems should be turned off during air leakage testing (NC Building Codes 2024).

For further details on InterNACHI standards, you can visit their website: [InterNACHI Standards of Practice](http://www.internachi.org/sop).

By adhering to these mandatory requirements and recommended practices, home inspectors can ensure a thorough and compliant inspection of heat pumps in North Carolina.
