## 1. Question

What does the InterNACHI SOP require for inspecting electrical panels?

### Answer

The InterNACHI Standards of Practice (SOP) outline specific requirements for inspecting electrical panels, which are complemented by the regulations from the NC Home Inspector Licensure Board. Here are the key points regarding the inspection of electrical panels:

### Mandatory Requirements from Regulatory Sources:
1. **Description of Electrical Service**: The home inspector must describe:
   - The electrical service amperage and voltage.
   - The electrical service entry conductor materials at the readily openable access panels at the main.
   - The type of electrical service (overhead or underground).
   - The location of main and distribution panels. (Source: NC Home Inspector Licensure Board)

2. **Reporting Deficiencies**: The inspector must report as needing correction:
   - Deficiencies in the integrity of service-entrance conductors’ insulation, drip loop, and vertical clearances.
   - Any unused circuit-breaker panel openings that are not filled.
   - The presence of solid conductor aluminum branch-circuit wiring, if readily visible.
   - Any tested receptacle with issues such as lack of power, incorrect polarity, missing cover, improper GFCI installation, evidence of arcing or excessive heat, or grounding issues.
   - The absence of smoke and/or carbon monoxide detectors. (Source: InterNACHI Standards of Practice)

3. **Limitations on Inspection**: The inspector is not required to:
   - Insert any tool, probe, or device into the main panelboard or sub-panels.
   - Test or operate arc-fault circuit interrupters or other overcurrent devices, except for ground fault circuit interrupters.
   - Dismantle any electrical device or control, other than removing the covers of panelboard enclosures. (Source: NC Home Inspector Licensure Board)

### Summary of InterNACHI SOP:
- The inspector shall describe the main service disconnect's amperage rating if labeled and the type of wiring observed.
- The inspector is not required to operate electrical systems that are shut down or to remove panelboard cabinet covers or dead fronts. (Source: InterNACHI Standards of Practice)

### Conclusion:
In summary, the inspection of electrical panels requires a description of key electrical service details and the reporting of specific deficiencies. However, inspectors must adhere to limitations regarding the tools and methods used during the inspection. For a comprehensive understanding, inspectors should refer to both the NC Home Inspector Licensure Board regulations and the InterNACHI Standards of Practice.

## 2. Question

What are the continuing education requirements for NC home inspectors?

### Answer

In North Carolina, the continuing education requirements for home inspectors are outlined by the NC Home Inspector Licensure Board. To renew an active home inspector license, the following requirements must be met:

1. **General Requirement**: Licensees must complete **12 credit hours** of continuing education during the license renewal period, which runs from **October 1 through the following September 30** (Source: NC Home Inspector Licensure Board, Section .1302).

2. **Specific Requirement for New Licensees**: Home inspectors who are licensed under G.S. 143-151.51(a)(5) and have not completed the pre-licensing education program or its equivalent must complete **16 credit hours** of continuing education for the first three years of licensure. This includes:
   - **Four hours** of a Board-approved course:
     - For the first year: A course on the **Standards of Practice and Report Writing**.
     - For the second and third years: A Board-approved course of the inspector's choosing.
   - **Four hours** of the update course component as described in Rule .1309 (Source: NC Home Inspector Licensure Board, Section .1302).

3. **Update Course Requirement**: To maintain an active license status, licensees must also complete a Board-developed update course within one year (Source: NC Home Inspector Licensure Board, Section .1309).

These requirements ensure that home inspectors stay informed about current practices and standards in the field. For further details, you may refer to the NC Home Inspector Licensure Board's regulations.

Additionally, it must provide a minimum diagonal measurement of 42 inches (1067 mm) from the top of the rail to the nosing of the nearest tread (NC Building Codes 2024).

What are the best practices for roof inspections according to InterNACHI and current industry standards?

### Answer

According to the InterNACHI Standards of Practice, the best practices for roof inspections include both mandatory requirements and recommended practices.

### Mandatory Requirements (InterNACHI Standards of Practice)

1. **Inspection Scope**:
   - Inspect from ground level or the eaves, covering:
     - Roof-covering materials
     - Gutters
     - Downspouts
     - Vents, flashing, skylights, chimney, and other roof penetrations
     - General structure of the roof from readily accessible panels, doors, or stairs (Source: InterNACHI Standards of Practice, Section 3.1).

2. **Description**:
   - Describe the type of roof-covering materials observed during the inspection (Source: InterNACHI Standards of Practice, Section 3.1.II).

3. **Reporting**:
   - Report any observed indications of active roof leaks as needing correction (Source: InterNACHI Standards of Practice, Section 3.1.III).

4. **Limitations**:
   - Inspectors are not required to:
     - Walk on any roof surface.
     - Predict the service life expectancy of the roof.
     - Inspect underground downspout diverter drainage pipes.
     - Remove snow, ice, debris, or other conditions that prohibit observation.
     - Move insulation.
     - Inspect antennae, satellite dishes, lightning arresters, or similar attachments.
     - Walk on any roof areas deemed unsafe or that might cause damage.
     - Perform a water test.
     - Warrant or certify the roof.
     - Confirm proper fastening or installation of any roof-covering material (Source: InterNACHI Standards of Practice, Section 3.1.IV).

## 7. Question

What are the inspection requirements for electrical, plumbing, and structural components in North Carolina?

### Answer

In North Carolina, the inspection requirements for electrical, plumbing, and structural components are governed by the NC Home Inspector Licensure Board and the NC Building Codes. Below is a summary of the mandatory inspection requirements for each component:

### Electrical Inspection Requirements
According to the NC Home Inspector Licensure Board, home inspectors are required to inspect the following electrical components:
1. **Electrical Service Entrance Conductors**: Inspect for proper installation and condition.
2. **Electrical Service Equipment**: This includes grounding equipment, main overcurrent devices, and the interiors of panelboard enclosures, unless unsafe conditions are reported.
3. **Amperage and Voltage Ratings**: Inspect the ratings of the electrical service.
4. **Branch Circuit Conductors**: Inspect their overcurrent devices and ensure compatibility of their ampacities at the interiors of panelboard enclosures, unless unsafe conditions are reported.
5. **Operation of Installed Fixtures**: Inspect a representative number of installed ceiling fans, lighting fixtures, and switches (NC Home Inspector Licensure Board, .1110 ELECTRICAL).

### Plumbing Inspection Requirements
The NC Home Inspector Licensure Board outlines that home inspectors must inspect:
1. **Pipes**: Ensure they are visible and properly installed.
2. **Water Heater Capacity**: Report on the adequacy of the reported water heater capacity.
3. **Thermal Expansion Tanks**: Report on their presence or absence.
4. **Sewage Pumps**: Inspect the operation of sewage pumps (NC Home Inspector Licensure Board, .1109 PLUMBING).

### Structural Inspection Requirements
The NC Building Codes specify that the following structural components must be visible for inspection:
1. **Pipes, Chimneys, and Vents**: Ensure these are accessible and properly installed.
2. **Flashing for Roofs and Chimneys**: Inspect for proper installation.
3. **Insulation Baffles**: Ensure they are in place.
4. **Lintels**: All required lintels must not be covered by any wall or ceiling finish material before approval (NC Building Codes 2024).

Additionally, special inspections may be required for certain types of work, and these must be performed by a North Carolina registered design professional or an inspector under their responsible charge (NC Building Codes 2024, Section 1704).

### Conclusion
In summary, home inspectors in North Carolina must adhere to specific inspection requirements for electrical, plumbing, and structural components as outlined by the NC Home Inspector Licensure Board and the NC Building Codes.    - Normal operating controls
   - Automatic safety controls
   - Chimneys, flues, and vents (where readily visible)
   - Heat distribution systems (including fans, pumps, ducts, piping, and related components)
   - The presence or absence of an installed heat source for each habitable space (NC Home Inspector Licensure Board, October 1, 2024).

2. **Description Requirements**: Inspectors are required to describe:
   - The energy source
   - The type of heating equipment and distribution system (NC Home Inspector Licensure Board).

3. **Operational Testing**: Inspectors must operate the heating systems using normal operating controls appropriate to the weather conditions at the time of the inspection (NC Home Inspector Licensure Board).

4. **Building Codes Compliance**: The NC Building Codes stipulate that calculations must account for indoor and outdoor temperatures and part-load ratios affecting the performance of heating, ventilating, and air-conditioning equipment (NC Building Codes 2024).

5. **Testing Protocols**: During testing, specific conditions must be met, such as:
   - Closing exterior windows and doors (but not sealing them)
   - Closing dampers (but not sealing them)
   - Keeping interior doors open
   - Ensuring heating and cooling systems are turned off during testing (NC Building Codes 2024).
